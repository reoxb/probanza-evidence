When this is worth paying for

Three situations where a measured audit changes the conversation.

You sell to regulated buyers.

Banks, healthcare, defense and the public sector now ask how your agents are governed. A third-party audit with published precision is an artifact their procurement can file. A slide is not.

Your agents hold real privilege.

A browser session, a shell, a shared memory layer, a payments API. The blast radius of a steered agent is the privilege it holds. We map agents to tools to resources and tell you where a hijacked instruction lands.

You ship weekly with a small security team.

Model calls whose output nothing validates. Prompts that quietly encode a pricing rule. Documents that can talk to your agent as if they were you. Ordinary code review does not catch these. Ours is built for them.

What you receive

Findings you can act on, and an honest map of what we did not see.

The differentiator is structural honesty. A zero from us means "we looked and there is nothing", never "we didn't look".

  • An applicability envelope: which rule families evaluated your repository, which report NOT EVALUATED, and why.

  • A walkthrough with your engineers: what is real, what is LOW by design, what one line of declarative config fixes.

How it works

From scope call to delivery in one to two weeks.

The run itself is fast. The value is in the pin, the triage and the conversation.

1

Scope call, 30 minutes. Repositories, size, what worries you, which package.

2

Read-only access at a pinned commit. A collaborator invite or a tarball. The pin is recorded, so the audit is reproducible against that exact SHA.

3

Run and human triage, 1 to 3 business days. Every finding is read by a person before it reaches you.

4

Delivery and walkthrough. For Audit Profundo, the 30-day re-audit closes the loop on your fixes.

Questions buyers ask before the scope call

Your code, our machine, your terms.

Who sees our code?

The people doing the audit at Divergia, and nobody else. The engine runs on our machine, not in your infrastructure and not as a hosted service. We read your repository once, at a commit you pin, from a read-only invite or a tarball you hand us.

Do you keep our repository?

We work from a read-only copy and record the pinned commit so the audit is reproducible. State your retention and access requirements at the scope call and we put them in writing before any access is granted.

Will you sign an NDA first?

Yes. If your process requires an NDA before access, we sign it before the scope call ends.

Does anything about our audit become public?

No. Client audits are private. The public evidence page measures the engine on open-source products only; your repository and your findings never appear there.

Is this a penetration test?

No. It is static analysis of your codebase: it finds exposure before it ships, it does not attempt attacks, and it declares what it did not evaluate. It complements a pentest; it does not replace one.

What if it finds nothing?

You still receive the applicability envelope: which rule families evaluated your repository and which reported NOT EVALUATED, and why. A zero from us means we looked. The price is the same.

Which codebases can you audit?

Python and TypeScript/JavaScript codebases, including agent frameworks and MCP tool manifests. Other languages are not supported today, and we tell you so before you pay.

What we will tell you upfront

A repository scan sees the code-shaped half of your risk. A default password on a live production account is a deployment fact, invisible to any static analysis, ours included. This is not a pentest and does not replace one. Our detector's registered boundaries are declared in the delivery, not papered over.
